\section{Executive Summary}

WaselX Express processes 3{,}500 daily orders across a 15-node delivery network spanning Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Sharjah, and Ajman. Our consulting engagement identified three critical operational inefficiencies costing an estimated \textbf{AED 3.4 million annually}. This report presents data-structure-driven solutions validated through working prototypes with measurable performance benchmarks.

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=WaselLight,colframe=WaselTeal,arc=4pt,boxrule=1pt]
\textbf{Key Findings:}
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Problem 1 --- Inefficient Routes (AED 1.2M/yr):} Dijkstra's algorithm reduces fleet distance by 18\%, saving AED~950{,}000 annually.
    \item \textbf{Problem 2 --- Slow Dispatch (12\% complaints):} Min-heap priority queue cuts dispatch latency from 8 minutes to under 90 seconds.
    \item \textbf{Problem 3 --- Poor Lookup (3.2/5 CSAT):} AVL tree guarantees O($\log n$) search --- under 100ms even at 15{,}000 orders/day.
\end{itemize}
\end{tcolorbox}

\textbf{Implementation Roadmap:} Phase~1 (Months~1--2): Priority queue dispatch system (AED~400K). Phase~2 (Months~3--5): Dijkstra route optimization (AED~800K). Phase~3 (Months~6--8): AVL tree migration. \textbf{Total projected benefit: AED~2.5M/year} against AED~1.2M implementation cost (2.1$\times$ ROI, 6-month payback).

\section{Task Area A: Graph Modeling, Shortest Paths \& MST}

\subsection{Q1 --- Graph Construction \textnormal{\small(10 Marks)}}

The WaselX delivery network comprises \textbf{15 nodes} (7 hubs + 8 delivery zones) connected by \textbf{24 bidirectional edges}, each carrying three weights: distance (km), time (min), and cost (AED). Both adjacency list ($O(V\!+\!E)$ space) and adjacency matrix ($O(V^2)$ space) representations were implemented.

\end{figure}

\subsection{Q2 --- Dijkstra's Algorithm: H1 $\rightarrow$ D1 \textnormal{\small(15 Marks)}}

Dijkstra's algorithm with a \textbf{from-scratch binary min-heap} computes the shortest path from Dubai Marina Hub (H1) to Downtown Dubai (D1). Result: direct route via Al Khail Rd at \textbf{8~km}, 15~min, 5.5~AED. Time complexity: $O((V\!+\!E)\log V)$ with $V\!=\!15$, $E\!=\!24$.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering

\end{figure}

\subsection{Q3 --- Floyd-Warshall: Hub All-Pairs \textnormal{\small(15 Marks)}}

Floyd-Warshall at $O(V^3)$ computed all-pairs shortest travel times across all 15 nodes. The 7$\times$7 hub sub-matrix reveals that the Abu Dhabi cluster (H5, H6) is \textbf{completely disconnected} from the Dubai--Sharjah network.

\subsection{Q5 --- CTO Memo: Dijkstra vs Floyd-Warshall \textnormal{\small(10 Marks)}}

\begin{memobox}{MEMORANDUM --- TO: CTO, WaselX Express | FROM: Group 3 | RE: Algorithm Selection}
For 3{,}500 daily source-to-destination routing queries, \textbf{Dijkstra's algorithm} at $O((V\!+\!E)\log V)$ per query is recommended as the real-time routing engine. Floyd-Warshall should be deployed as a \textbf{nightly batch job} for hub connectivity analytics at $O(V^3)$. At current network size ($V\!=\!15$), Floyd-Warshall's 3{,}375 operations are trivial; however, Dijkstra scales better if the network grows beyond 100 nodes.
\end{memobox}

\subsection{Q11 --- Scaling to 15{,}000 Orders \textnormal{\small(8 Marks)}}

The sorted array bottleneck occurs at ${\sim}$8{,}000--10{,}000 active orders due to $O(n)$ insertion cost. Migration to AVL tree recommended with a three-phase shadow-index rollout: (1) parallel AVL build, (2) read verification, (3) write cutover.

% ═══════════════ TASK AREA C ═══════════════
\section{Task Area C: Linked Lists, Queues \& Order Pipeline}

\subsection{Q12 --- Doubly Linked List \textnormal{\small(10 Marks)}}
Rider delivery routes modeled as a DLL with $O(1)$ mid-route insertion/deletion, supporting forward and reverse traversal for route optimization and backtracking.

\subsection{Q13 --- Circular Linked List \textnormal{\small(8 Marks)}}
Round-robin rider assignment using a circular linked list with 8 riders. Handles rider removal mid-rotation seamlessly, maintaining fair load distribution.

\subsection{Q14 --- Priority Queue with Min-Heap \textnormal{\small(12 Marks)}}
From-scratch min-heap with \texttt{(priority, arrival\_index, order)} tuple keys ensuring \textbf{FIFO fairness} within equal-priority tiers. VIP Same-Hour orders dispatch before Economy, but two Economy orders with the same priority preserve arrival order.

\subsection{Q15 --- Stack for Order Lifecycle \textnormal{\small(8 Marks)}}
LIFO stack tracks order state transitions (Received $\rightarrow$ Processing $\rightarrow$ Dispatched $\rightarrow$ Delivered) with \texttt{undo()} capability for accidental status changes.

\subsection{Q16 --- Dispatch Pipeline Design \textnormal{\small(10 Marks)}}
Per-zone FIFO queues with priority pre-sort recommended over a single global queue. A priority escalation mechanism promotes starved orders after configurable wait thresholds.

\subsection{Q17 --- Leadership Brief \textnormal{\small(10 Marks)}}

\begin{memobox}{TO: CEO | FROM: Head of Delivery Operations | RE: Priority Queue Implementation}
The priority dispatch system addresses VIP customer churn (23\% of subscribers lost in Q4~2025). Projected impact: 15\% VIP recovery = \textbf{AED~315{,}000/year} in retained revenue. Total implementation cost: AED~400{,}000 with 15-month payback.

% ═══════════════ TASK AREA D ═══════════════
\section{Task Area D: Sorting, Searching \& Divide and Conquer}

\subsection{Q18 --- Merge Sort \& Quick Sort \textnormal{\small(12 Marks)}}
Two-key sort (Zone alphabetical, Priority ascending) on 15 orders. Merge Sort preferred for \textbf{stability} --- preserving FIFO arrival order within identical sort keys.

\subsection{Q19 --- Binary Search vs Linear Search \textnormal{\small(10 Marks)}}
Binary Search finds Order~10347 in ${\sim}$10 comparisons versus 347 for Linear Search. At 500 daily customer support lookups: \textbf{96\% reduction} in total comparisons (5{,}000 vs 250{,}000).

\subsection{Q20 --- Peak Hour Detection \textnormal{\small(10 Marks)}}
Divide-and-conquer approach correctly identifies peak ordering hour from 6{,}000 Ramadan orders. Honest assessment: both D\&C and linear scan are $O(n)$ for this problem --- D\&C provides no speedup when every element must be examined.

\subsection{Q21 --- Sorting Recommendation \textnormal{\small(8 Marks)}}
\textbf{Merge Sort} recommended for nightly batch job (50{,}000 partially-sorted records): guaranteed $O(n\log n)$, stable, and naturally suited to pre-sorted runs within zones. Quick Sort's $O(n^2)$ worst case on partially-sorted data is unacceptable for production SLAs.

This project demonstrates that well-chosen data structures and algorithms directly address WaselX Express's three critical challenges. The projected \textbf{AED~2.5M annual benefit} against AED~1.2M implementation cost makes this a compelling technology investment with clear competitive advantages in the UAE last-mile delivery market.
